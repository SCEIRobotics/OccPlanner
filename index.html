03 / Overview

The pixel goal says where to go.
Occupancy reveals how to get there safely.

Planning · OccPlanner

OccPlanner is a goal-aware occupancy-conditioned diffusion planner for pixel-goal navigation. Given an RGB-D observation history and a pixel goal, it jointly learns local 3D occupancy and continuous future trajectories. Auxiliary ego-goal supervision grounds the goal in robot-centric metric space, while temporal context and compact near-ground occupancy tokens condition trajectory generation.

Supervision · L3ROcc

L3ROcc is a training-data generation pipeline that converts monocular navigation videos into temporally consistent, robot-centric occupancy, visibility, and trajectory annotations through geometric reconstruction, metric alignment, voxelization, and visibility-aware ray marching.

04 / OccPlanner

Goal-Aware Occupancy-Conditioned Diffusion Planner.

OccPlanner is a continuous planner for pixel-goal navigation. A shared π³-based RGB-D geometric encoder extracts spatiotemporal features from the observation history; the occupancy branch predicts voxel occupancy and distills near-ground features into compact occupancy tokens. Auxiliary ego-goal supervision grounds the pixel goal in robot-centric metric space, after which goal-context and goal-occupancy interactions construct a goal- and geometry-aware state representation that conditions DDPM-based trajectory generation.

Training objectives. OccPlanner combines a focal voxel-occupancy loss, a Smooth L1 diffusion-noise loss, and an auxiliary ego-goal regression loss. PointGoal supervision is used only during training.

05 / L3ROcc

Local 3D Reconstruction with Occupancy.

L3ROcc creates the 3D supervision used to train OccPlanner. Starting from a monocular RGB navigation video, it reconstructs scene geometry and camera motion with π³, recovers approximate metric scale when reference poses are available, and aligns the geometry, camera poses, and viewing rays with the robot frame. Voxelization and visibility-aware ray marching then distinguish occupied, ray-observed free, and unobserved space while preserving the aligned camera trajectory.

01

Reconstruct

Recover shared scene geometry, local pointmaps, confidence maps, sparse camera poses, and viewing rays with π³, then densify the camera trajectory.

02

Align

Estimate an approximately metric scene scale when reference poses are available, then transform geometry, poses, and viewing rays into the robot base frame.

03

Label

Voxelize aligned local geometry into candidate occupancy. Ray marching labels space before the first hit as free, the first hit as occupied, and space behind it as unobserved.

Training only. L3ROcc outputs sparse visible-occupancy sequences, packed binary visibility masks, and aligned camera trajectory metadata. It is not required during OccPlanner inference.

06 / Results

Obstacle-aware trajectories in simulation and the real world.

We evaluate closed-loop navigation on the same fixed set of 6,000 episodes across 60 unseen indoor scenes, covering 3–5 m and 5–8 m start–goal ranges and reporting SR, SPL, and DTG. In the real world, we study zero-shot transfer and fine-tuning in open loop, then demonstrate physical closed-loop navigation with the fine-tuned model on a Unitree Go2.

Simulation

Consistent pixel-goal grounding across diverse scene layouts.

Across home, commercial, cluttered-easy, and cluttered-hard scenes, OccPlanner predicts smooth trajectories that remain within inferred traversable regions while avoiding observed obstacles. Its larger gains under long-range and cluttered settings indicate improved robustness to extended planning horizons and complex local geometry.

Real world

From open-loop adaptation to physical closed-loop navigation.

We first compare the simulator-trained and real-world fine-tuned models in an open-loop setting using the same RGB-D observations and pixel goals. We then deploy the fine-tuned OccPlanner on a Unitree Go2 for qualitative physical closed-loop navigation in a cluttered office.
